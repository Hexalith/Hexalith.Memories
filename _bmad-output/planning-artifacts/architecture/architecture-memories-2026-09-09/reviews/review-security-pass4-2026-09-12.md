**Spine:** `:106` (AD-5 *Binds*: "Dapr ingress and pub/sub delivery … and the operator artifact"), `:108` (the artifact holds "the **CloudEvent-`source`-prefix-to-tenant routing map**"; and, four sentences later, "Dapr pub/sub ingress derives tenant authority from the delivery channel and **never from the envelope**: the receiving component or topic is bound to one tenant, **or the operator artifact's routing map resolves the delivery's authenticated channel to one tenant**, and the map is matched **ordinally** under AD-23's grammar rather than case-insensitively"), `:107` (*Prevents*: "Request fields, **envelope fields** … from serving as authorization"), `:321` (Operational Boundaries: the artifact is "the single home for … the pub/sub routing map"), row `:386`. **Code:** `TenantEventRoutingOptions.cs:19-21` — `SourceToTenantMap`, "Longest-prefix wins, case-insensitive," unknown sources "dropped with a warning." **Requirement:** `prd.md:1141` (NFR8).

**What a compliant implementation permits.** A CloudEvent `source` prefix is not a channel. The artifact is *defined* as holding a source-prefix-to-tenant map; the rule that consumes it says the map resolves "the delivery's authenticated channel." Those are two different inputs, and only one of them is authenticated. An implementer building the artifact the spine defines writes a map whose keys are `source` prefixes and matches the delivered envelope's `source` against them — which is tenant authority derived from an envelope field, forbidden verbatim eight lines above. An implementer building the rule's safe reading binds each component or topic to one tenant and never consults a `source` prefix at all, at which point the artifact's third fact is dead weight. The architecture does not say which, and the ledger entrenches the unsafe one: row `:386`'s convergence asks only to "Match the routing map ordinally, reject a delivery that resolves to no tenant or to an inactive one, and remove or gate the auto-provision path," leaving the key domain exactly as shipped.

**Scenario 1 — publisher-selected tenant.** Two workloads publish to the shared topic. The operator artifact maps `https://acme.example/` → `acme` and `https://globex.example/` → `globex`. A workload authorized to publish sets `source: https://globex.example/orders` and publishes a unit. The subscriber resolves `globex` from the map, the resolved tenant is `Active`, and the envelope tenant matches the resolution, so no rejection clause fires. The unit is ingested into `globex`, projected into `globex`'s indexes and graph, and is returned to `globex`'s users with provenance. That is a cross-tenant write chosen by the publisher, permitted by the artifact the architecture defines. The channel is authenticated; the field that selected the tenant is not.

**Scenario 2 — prefix ambiguity has no tie-break.** Prefix matching over a non-grammar field is not injective. The spine states neither longest-prefix-wins nor any other rule, so with `https://acme.example/` → `acme` and `https://acme.example/legacy/` → `acme-legacy` in one artifact, a delivery from `https://acme.example/legacy/orders` resolves to two tenants and the choice is the implementer's. The shipped code answers longest-prefix-wins (`TenantEventRoutingOptions.cs:19`); the architecture does not, and the two tenants are different isolation domains.

**Scenario 3 — "matched ordinally under AD-23's grammar" is not applicable to this key.** AD-23's grammar governs identifiers this platform issues. A `source` is a URI reference containing `://`, mixed case, and path separators, none of which the grammar admits, so "under AD-23's grammar" attaches a property to a value the grammar excludes. Meanwhile AD-4 `:102` canonicalizes `source` once at ingress by lowercasing scheme and host — so an artifact authored with any uppercase in a host will silently never match after canonicalization, and the deployment fails closed by accident rather than by rule (the shipped code's "dropped with a warning" is the fail-open version of the same ambiguity).

**Why high and not critical.** The clause contains the correct rule — tenant authority from the channel, never the envelope — in the same sentence. A reviewer who reads only that half builds a safe system, and NFR8 as written is not defeated by a document that states the right rule and an inconsistent artifact definition. It is high because a fully compliant reading of the artifact's own definition produces a cross-tenant write.

**Closing text (`:108`, replacing the routing-map fact and its consuming clause).** *"…and the delivery-channel-to-tenant routing map, whose keys are pub/sub components, topics, or authenticated publisher identities and never CloudEvent envelope fields. Dapr pub/sub ingress derives tenant authority from the delivery channel and never from the envelope: the receiving component or topic is bound to one tenant, or the operator artifact's map resolves the delivery's authenticated channel identity to exactly one tenant by whole-value ordinal match with no prefix, wildcard, or case-insensitive matching. A CloudEvent `source` is provenance under AD-13 and is never an input to tenant resolution."* Amend row `:386` to require replacing the key domain, not only the comparison.

### SEC4-02 — high — AD-21 admits writes to the platform partition from one principal, and three of the four things it holds are written by other principals

**Spine:** `:204` ("It holds AD-16's erased-tenant register, the export-bundle index, erasure completion evidence, and AD-6's content-free lifecycle-state projection" … "**Reading and writing it is an authorization decision:** writes are admitted **only from the erasure workflow's operator-scoped principal** and are append-only" … "A tombstone written in error is corrected by **an operator procedure** that appends a recorded reversal"), `:174` (AD-16: "each bundle is **registered at creation** in AD-21's bundle index"), `:114` (AD-6: "a content-free projection of each tenant's identifier, current state, and transition time is **mirrored onto AD-21's platform partition**", where "**Every transition** is an AD-2-committed domain event"), `:180` (AD-17: "the mapping is destroyed when the last record it addresses is purged — **at which point purge completion for that tenant is recorded in AD-21's register**"), `:107` (*Prevents*: "two writers of one authorization decision"), row `:384`.

**What a compliant implementation permits.** One sentence names one admitted writer. Four artifact classes live on the partition and three of them are produced outside the erasure workflow:

1. **The bundle index**, written "at creation" of an export bundle — an ordinary tenant-authority operation under FR71, not an erasure.
2. **The lifecycle-state projection**, written on *every* transition including `Provisioning` and `Active` — by AD-6's lifecycle workflow, which is a different workflow with a different operator-scoped principal.
3. **Telemetry purge completion**, written by Platform Operations' operator-scoped principal under AD-17's third exemption.

An implementer has three ways out and each is a defect the architecture elsewhere forbids. **(a)** Honor `:204` literally: bundles are never registered, so `:174`'s "Import admits a bundle only when its digest is present in the index" admits nothing and FR71's round-trip is dead; the lifecycle projection is never written, so AD-6's shred-surviving state does not survive and `:108`'s check 3 has nothing to read after erasure. **(b)** Broaden the admitted writer set to "operator-scoped principals" generally — which makes every lifecycle and Platform Operations principal a writer to the store that is the sole authority for tenant-ID non-reuse and erasure completion. Append-only still prevents deletion, but it does not prevent *appending*: a broadened writer can append a spurious tombstone (an irreversible denial of a tenant identifier, SEC3-05 scenario 2 returning through a wider door) or a spurious completion-evidence record, which is the one artifact NFR16's operator evidence rests on. **(c)** Route every platform-partition write through the erasure workflow, so a bundle export and an ordinary `Provisioning` transition must invoke the erasure workflow — which AD-5 `:108` enumerates as an exempt operation, widening the exemption AD-5 just closed.

**The reversal remedy has the same hole.** `:204`'s sanctioned correction is "an operator procedure that appends a recorded reversal." Under the same sentence's write rule, the only admitted writer is the erasure workflow's principal, so the remedy's writer is not admitted — the corrective path that exists to answer SEC3-05's write-once poison case is unimplementable as written.

**Closing text (`:204`, replacing the write sentence).** *"**Reading and writing it is an authorization decision, stated per record class.** Erased-tenant tombstones and erasure completion evidence are written only by AD-16's erasure workflow under its operator-scoped principal; the lifecycle-state projection only by AD-6's lifecycle workflow under its operator-scoped principal; the bundle index only by the export and erasure operations, each under the principal the operator artifact enumerates for it; telemetry purge completion only by AD-17's Platform Operations principal. No principal may write a class the artifact does not enumerate for it. Every write is append-only — EventStore's append-only semantics are the enforcement, not a convention — so no principal, application or operator, can modify or delete a record of any class. A tombstone written in error is corrected by an operator procedure that appends a recorded reversal under an operator principal the artifact enumerates for that procedure, never by a delete."* Extend row `:384` to require the per-class writer table.

### SEC4-05 — medium — The exemption's one-tenant binding is enforced only by the caller, and the extended NFR8 assertion is scoped to the operations the binding does not cover

**Spine:** `:108` ("scoped at initiation to exactly one tenant identifier recorded in its **lifecycle evidence**, which it may not change on resume or retry"; the receiver "accepts it only for an enumerated operation, only from an app ID the artifact marks operator-scoped, and **only for the single tenant the call names**"; "the isolation suite authenticates as an operator principal and asserts that every **non-exempt** operation it attempts against a tenant is refused"; "Its **membership at provisioning** is the set of app IDs the operator artifact declares for that tenant; adding or removing an app ID on a live tenant is an AD-6 grant-amendment lifecycle operation"), row `:388` ("assert that every **non-enumerated** operation it attempts against a tenant is refused, and that no enumerated operation touches a tenant in the register").

**What a compliant implementation permits.** The binding and its enforcement point are in different places. The tenant a workflow may act on lives in that workflow's own lifecycle evidence; the receiver of an enumerated call checks that the call names *a* single tenant, not that it names *that* tenant, because the receiver cannot see the caller's lifecycle evidence. So the one-tenant property is a rule the caller keeps about itself, with no independent check — and the verification method that was extended to cover operator principals asserts only that **non-exempt / non-enumerated** operations are refused, which is the complement of the set where this property matters. A green NFR8 run says nothing about whether an enumerated operation can be invoked for a tenant outside the invoking workflow's scope.

**Grant amendment compounds it.** Provisioning's grant membership is pinned to what the artifact declares for that tenant. Amendment is not: `:108` makes it a lifecycle operation and constrains who may invoke it, never which app ID it may pair with the tenant. A compliant amendment can therefore grant full tenant authority to any `system:*` app ID with no artifact declaration, no second writer, and no wildcard decision — the mint SEC3-01 identified, narrowed from "any tenant" to "one tenant, any app ID."

**Closing text (`:108`).** *"A receiving service admits an enumerated operation only when the tenant the call names equals the tenant recorded in the caller's lifecycle evidence for that workflow instance, which the call carries as a verifiable reference to that evidence rather than as a caller-set value; a mismatch fails closed. A grant amendment may add only an app ID the operator artifact declares for that tenant, and removing an app ID the artifact no longer declares is the only other admissible amendment. The isolation suite asserts both: that a non-enumerated operation by an operator principal is refused, and that an enumerated operation naming a tenant outside the caller's recorded scope is refused."* Extend row `:388` to name the second assertion.

### SEC4-06 — medium — A register restored from backup cannot detect its own staleness on the provisioning path

**Spine:** `:204` ("an absent marker, an unreachable partition, a read error, or **a restore older than the newest erasure it records** are all **unavailable**"; "The register carries a monotonic sequence advanced on every write and never reset; every backup, export bundle, and EventStore restore artifact records the sequence current at its creation, and **a restore is admitted only when the live register's sequence is greater than or equal to the artifact's**. A register restored from backup **resumes at the highest sequence it can prove**, and an artifact stamped higher fails closed until the register is reconciled"), `:114` (provisioning consults it "failing closed when the register is … older than the newest erasure it records"). **Requirement:** `prd.md:1052` (FR39: "the deleted tenant ID cannot be reused").

**What a compliant implementation permits.** The staleness test is self-referential: "older than the newest erasure **it records**" is a condition a rolled-back register cannot evaluate, because the erasures it no longer records are precisely the ones it cannot see. The sequence mechanism supplies a real comparand for *restore and import admission* — an artifact stamped 9 against a register at 5 fails closed, correctly — and supplies none for **provisioning**, which compares against nothing and is told the register is available because the marker reads back and the register "resumes at the highest sequence it can prove."

**Scenario.** The platform partition is restored from a backup taken at sequence 5. Tombstones 6 through 9, including tenant `claims`, were written after it. Readiness passes: the marker reads back. An operator provisions `claims`. The register is consulted, is available, and does not contain `claims`. Provisioning proceeds, and the new tenant legitimately owns every ACL pattern, index name, graph name, and secret path derived from that identifier — so any surviving artifact of the erased tenant (see SEC4-03) becomes readable by the new one under a fully authorized principal. FR39's non-reuse guarantee is defeated with no rule violated.

**Closing text (`:204`).** *"A register restored from backup is **unavailable** until it is reconciled: reconciliation compares its highest proven sequence against the highest sequence recorded in any surviving stamped artifact and against the platform-bootstrap marker's last observed value, which readiness records outside the partition, and the register remains unavailable — blocking provisioning, deletion, replay, and every restore — until an operator records the reconciliation outcome as an append. A register that cannot prove it is at or beyond the last sequence observed by readiness is stale, not empty."*

### SEC4-07 — medium — The platform partition's read rule is stated per partition while it holds four classes of differing sensitivity, and one of them is content-derived material retained forever

**Spine:** `:204` ("reads are admitted to the enumerated consult paths and to operator principals"; "never carrying tenant content"), `:174` (the bundle index carries "its tenant, issuance time, and **content digest**"; and AD-16's purge scope is "every store that can hold tenant content **or content-derived material**"), `:114` (authorization decisions "read the committed state or that projection"), `:225` (indistinguishability on scoped resources).

**What a compliant implementation permits.** Two consequences of one granularity choice.

**(a) Read scope.** `:114` lets any authorization decision read the lifecycle-state projection, and after crypto-shredding that projection is the only readable copy — so every service that authorizes a call is a reader of the partition. `:204` admits reads only to "the enumerated consult paths and to operator principals," so either the authorization path is not a reader (and post-erasure state is unreadable where it is needed) or it is, and every authorizing service can also read the erased-tenant register and the bundle index. The register's read surface is the SEC2-16 disclosure at scale (which tenant identifiers ever existed and were erased); the bundle index's is worse, below.

**(b) The digest.** A bundle content digest is content-derived material by AD-16's own definition, and it lives on a partition that is "never crypto-shredded." After erasure it is a permanent confirmation oracle: anyone who can read the index and holds a candidate bundle can prove that bundle was the erased tenant's, and can prove it for as long as the platform exists. AD-21 asserts the partition never carries tenant content; AD-16's vocabulary says content-derived material is in scope wherever it lives. The two decisions disagree about what a digest is, and the disagreement is load-bearing because the index must survive erasure to do its job.

**Closing text (`:204`).** *"Read admission is stated per record class: the lifecycle-state projection is readable by every authorization path; the erased-tenant register by the enumerated consult paths and operator principals; the bundle index and erasure completion evidence by operator principals and the import path alone. A bundle's recorded digest is content-derived material and is salted per platform so it cannot confirm a candidate bundle's content without the platform secret; the index entry is otherwise content-free. A refusal caused by the register or the index is reported under the Conventions' indistinguishable-response rule."*

### SEC4-14 — low — The legacy-remediation scope is understated: the pre-grammar population is the entire installed population, and remediation changes identifiers two decisions require to be stable

**Spine:** `:216` ("Identifiers issued before the grammar existed are enumerated and remediated including case-variant resolution … a legacy identifier whose lowercase form collides with an issued one is **re-issued** rather than normalized"), row `:375`, `:156` (AD-13: "compare `MemoryUnitId` only as an opaque **stable** string"), `:222` (Conventions: "opaque **stable** strings"). **Code:** `CaseService.cs:86` and `:152` mint uppercase ULIDs for case ids and annotation MemoryUnitIds; `TenantIdGuard.cs:41` admits mixed-case tenant identifiers.

**Closing text (row `:375` convergence).** *"…and state the migration's shape: the sweep is over the entire issued corpus, not a legacy tail; a case-insensitively unique identifier is lowercased in place, which is injective; an identifier that must be re-issued carries a recorded old-to-new mapping that every derived key family, provenance record, and retained telemetry reference is rewritten against, and the mapping is itself content-free and is retained for as long as any record bearing the old value."*
