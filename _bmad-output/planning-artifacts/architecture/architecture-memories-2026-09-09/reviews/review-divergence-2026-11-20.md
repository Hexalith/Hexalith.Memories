# Architecture consistency review · November 20, 2026

**Verdict: pass after correction.** The revised AD-3 and alignment ledger now require authoritative replay on projection loss or inconsistency. Stale acknowledgements cannot regress a completed tuple. The final AD-20 requires the NFR31 walkthrough to finish in under 30 minutes, including Aspire installation and image pulls. The review rechecked all three edits against the rule text and the PRD. Release qualification remains open under AD-20.

| Initial severity | Decisions | How the draft could diverge | Verified correction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High | AD-2, AD-3, AD-16 | An operator could treat AD-3’s “derived-store restore” as permission to restore a shared Redis or FalkorDB snapshot and invalidate its checkpoint. The recovery team following AD-2 would rebuild those same projections from accepted events. | AD-3 names projection loss or inconsistency as the reason to write `reprojectionRequired`. The corresponding alignment row and operational summary assign rebuilding to authoritative replay. The recovery runbook also requires keeping a partial or uncertain logical import offline. |
| High | AD-3 | One status consumer could regress an already completed active revision after a late failure or wrong-generation acknowledgement. A second consumer following the monotonic tuple rule would retain the completed revision. Case activity and counters would then disagree. | AD-3 scopes each acknowledgement to its admitted revision, generation and epoch. A stale or mismatched acknowledgement cannot change a newer checkpoint or regress a completed tuple. An active tuple with missing current acknowledgements remains in progress or reports its own actionable failure. |
| Medium | AD-20, G3 | “Within 30 minutes” admits a run recorded at exactly 30:00. The PRD’s G3 and NFR31 require a strictly shorter elapsed time. | AD-20 specifies under 30 minutes on the NFR31 clean-machine profile and includes the manual README/AppHost path through the enrolled CLI. |
