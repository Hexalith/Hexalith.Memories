## What Went Well

### Evidence became more trustworthy

- Story 26.3 eliminated false-green integration stubs. Unsupported scenarios are now explicit skips with structured deferred-work records rather than silent passes.
- Story 26.4 preserved the benchmark failure as a real blocking result. The lane works precisely because it reports the product-quality miss rather than weakening the thesis or reproducibility checks.
- Review repeatedly converted substring, one-way, or fail-open checks into exact structural and end-state verification.

### Production operations became executable

- Deployment is reproducible from repository-owned OCI and Kustomize artifacts and was exercised through a disposable kind/Dapr rollout.
- Backup and restore now cover logical snapshot import, graph reconstruction, re-derived semantic vectors, restart survival, and operational recovery procedures.
- Six operational runbooks are source-tied and machine-checked rather than relying on unverified prose.

### Real execution created useful corrective scope

- Release authentication failure produced Story 26.6 and aligned the publisher with the shared Hexalith Zot contract.
- Restart failure produced Story 26.7, exposed hidden nested HTTP retry/timeout behavior, and strengthened replay-safe counter recovery.
- The corrective stories retained the original safety constraints: no raised timeout, removed restart, suppressed failure, or weakened zero-loss assertion was used to obtain green evidence.

## Challenges and Growth Areas

### Review entry was weaker than the operational risk

All seven stories required review-driven hardening. Weak or fail-open verification recurred in at least Stories 26.1, 26.3, 26.4, and 26.5. Story 26.5 accepted 54 findings across its review chunks, showing that procedures could appear complete before they were sufficiently executable, bounded, fail-closed, and evidence-preserving.

The systemic issue is a mismatch between review entry criteria and operational risk. Realistic execution and exact outcome verification often occurred after an artifact had already entered review.

### Planning and evidence traceability drifted

- `epic-26-context.md` contains only Stories 26.1 through 26.5.
- Stories 26.6 and 26.7 are present in the epic and sprint status but do not have canonical dedicated story records.
- Some story frontmatter and final status evidence disagree.
- The epic row remains `in-progress` while all story rows are `done`; this is currently appropriate because the benchmark closure blocker remains open, but the reason must remain explicit.

The lesson is not to avoid emergent work. When corrective scope joins an epic, epics, context, story records, sprint status, and deferred-work artifacts must be synchronized together.

### Quality headroom and deferred work remain constrained

- Coverage passes by 0.15 percentage points, leaving little margin above the ratchet.
- Eight integration scenarios remain accepted skips.
- The representative 512 MiB / approximately 100K-unit restore-boundary proof remains deferred.
- Deployment hardening remains for non-root data stores, network policy/PSS, Dapr configuration drift, application image provenance, and Data Protection behavior under a read-only root filesystem.

These are governed residuals, not hidden failures, but they require continuing ownership and explicit reopen triggers.

## Key Insights

1. **Story completion and epic readiness are distinct states.** A hard product gate keeps the epic open even when every story is done.
2. **Operational evidence belongs before review.** Deployment, restore, restart, and runbook work should enter review only with realistic execution evidence.
3. **A guard must prove the intended semantic outcome.** Running and passing a check is insufficient when the check can pass without exercising the requirement.
4. **Emergent stories require atomic traceability updates.** Corrective scope is incomplete until its planning and evidence records agree.
5. **Accepted debt must stay actionable.** Every skip or residual needs an owner, disposition, and executable reopen trigger.
