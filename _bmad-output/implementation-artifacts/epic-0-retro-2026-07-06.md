## Epic Review

### What Went Well

- The planning correction made Epic 0 a readable foundation path: scaffold, tenant provisioning, case bootstrap, validation guard, then data-writing Epic 1 work.
- The epic definition of done is crisp and safety-oriented. It requires active tenant infrastructure, an active case, and pre-backend validation for invalid tenant/case scope.
- Ownership boundaries are explicit. Story 0.1 does not duplicate the full tenant lifecycle owned by Story 5.1, and Story 0.2 does not absorb broader case management owned by Epic 3.
- The project preserved historical traceability instead of pretending the work was implemented under the new numbering from the start.
- The readiness reports confirm Epic 1 can function using Epic 0 output, and later implementation-readiness cleanup kept Story 0.4 as imported historical evidence rather than a future dependency.

### What Was Difficult

- The evidence is fragmented. Story 0.0 points to historical Story 1.1, Story 0.2 points to Story 3.1, and Story 0.4 points to Story 11.1.
- Story 0.1 and Story 0.3 are recorded as completed prerequisite slices, but their reconciliation artifacts are thinner than the deep implementation stories they depend on.
- Several early broad technical stories are explicitly marked as historical patterns that should not be repeated. That is the right governance call, but it means future agents must avoid using Epic 1 historical story shape as a template.
- Build/test confidence for Story 0.4 is imported from Story 11.1. That is valid per the planning amendment, but it requires careful wording so it is not misread as a hidden dependency on later operational work.

### Key Insights

1. Retrospective traceability matters most when stories are renumbered or reclassified after implementation.
2. Foundation gates need both status rows and evidence maps. A `done` row is not enough when the proof lives in historical story files.
3. Tenant and case safety are architectural invariants, not local implementation details. Every ingestion, search, graph, CLI, and MCP path must keep validating those invariants.
4. Historical broad slices should remain evidence, not templates. New work should use smaller vertical stories with observable proof.
