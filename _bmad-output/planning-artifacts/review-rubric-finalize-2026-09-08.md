## Overall verdict

This PRD now works as a decision instrument: one thesis (weighted RRF over available axes beats BM25+semantic), one falsifier whose numbers live in the PRD (G1: N ≥ 50, ΔNDCG@10 ≥ 0.02, κ ≥ 0.6), a dated release record with real no-go outcomes, and delivery-status registers that admit what is stubbed. Glossary, ingestion vocabulary, FR/NFR IDs, and the Phase 1 CLI surface table are safe for downstream extraction, and every code reference checked in the repo except one is accurate. What is at risk is the execution of the record it just built: L3 names a fixture (`samples/02-eventstore-integration/`) that does not exist and carries no delivery status; the kill switch leaves "freeze FalkorDB *or* cut it" and the fate of the L-gates after a G1 failure undecided; and nine FRs marked **Shipped** have no developer surface an engineer can locate from the PRD. None of these re-open a settled decision — they are the residue of implementing those decisions inconsistently in the text.

## Decision-readiness — strong

A decision-maker can act. § Measurable Outcomes states the thesis-gate protocol with pre-registered numbers and names the diagnostic for what it is ("N=8, every query is tagged as requiring all three axes, the corpus and labels are synthetic, the control is the best *single* axis"). The Release decision record gives each gate an owner, a date, and a failure outcome that is not a slip ("**No launch.** MCP and EventStore packages stay marked preview and are not announced; the date is not moved without a sprint-change proposal"). Rejected alternatives are recorded (magnitude-blend, atomic triple-write, `tenant switch`, "retitle the 80% line as diagnostic only") with the reason each lost. Open Questions 1 and 2 are the two things that actually block G1 (dates; reviewers and frozen corpus) and both have owners and revisit dates.

Where the record is still soft is at its two hinges: what the kill switch decides about the graph if G1 fails, and whether L3 can be evaluated at all on 2026-11-30. Both are stated as if settled and are not.

### Findings

- **high** L3 rests on a fixture that does not exist and has no delivery status (§ Measurable Outcomes › Causal Chain Completeness; § Release decision record › L1–L3; § Developer Experience & Documentation › In-Repo Examples) — "the Phase 1.5 sample, `samples/02-eventstore-integration/`, is the fixture of record" and L3 is "Causal chain completeness ≥95% on the named known-chain fixture". There is no `samples/` directory anywhere in the repository (checked at root and recursively outside `references/` and `_bmad-output/`), and no known-chain fixture in `tests/`. The In-Repo Examples table lists three samples and is the only inventory in this PRD without a delivery-status column, so a reader of the record ("Epics 9–10 code delivered; gates not evaluated") will assume L3 is evaluable today. It is not. *Fix:* Add a delivery status to the samples table (all three: not started, or name the epic/story), and in the L3 row and Causal Chain Completeness paragraph write "fixture to be created under Epic N before 2026-11-30" — or point L3 at a fixture that exists.
- **high** The kill switch contains an undecided "or" and does not say what happens to the L-gates after a G1 failure (§ Measurable Outcomes › kill switch; § Release decision record) — "remove graph from default `axes=hybrid`; freeze FalkorDB for causal traversal **or** cut it from general search". Those are two different products: one keeps the Phase 1.5 causal surface alive on a non-default axis, the other removes the graph store that L3 depends on. The G1 row's failure outcome ("no Phase 1.5 launch decision is taken") does not say whether the L1–L3 row is cancelled, deferred by SCP, or still evaluated on 2026-11-30. Because G1 tests the graph on its weakest population by design (`contains` / `references` / `annotates` on files — § Phase 1 graph inventory) and its failure blocks the launch of a causal surface whose population was never scored, the PRD should state that asymmetry is deliberate and what evidence, if any, may inform "freeze vs cut". *Fix:* Replace "or" with the decision (recommended: freeze for causal traversal, cut from default hybrid; cutting FalkorDB entirely requires an SCP). Add one sentence to the L1–L3 row: "If G1 fails on 2026-10-31 this row is suspended; re-activation requires a sprint-change proposal." Optionally allow one named diagnostic (hybrid vs two-axis on the L3 fixture) to be run before the freeze/cut choice, and say it cannot pass G1.
- **medium** G3's path is ambiguous between `quickstart` and the Journey 9 manual path while the manual path's verbs are stubs (§ Release decision record › G1–G5 "G3 rests on the Story 7.4 quickstart walkthrough"; NFR31 "AppHost → first CLI search"; § Journey 9; § Phase 1 CLI surface) — Journey 9 is named "**Phase 1 thesis success path**" and runs `memories case create` and `memories ingest ./sample-claims/`, both "**Not started** — `NotImplementedCommand` stub". `memories quickstart` is shipped and performs "sample tenant → sample ingest → validation search". The NFR delivery status says NFR31 has "no timed clean-machine record". So G3 either passes today on `quickstart` alone (and Journey 9 is not the G3 path), or it needs the stubs (and the stubs are on the 2026-10-31 critical path). The PRD does not say which. *Fix:* In NFR31 or the G3 row, state the gate's path explicitly: "G3 is timed on the README manual path (case create → ingest → search); `quickstart` is the scripted convenience, not the gate" — or the reverse. If manual, add "blocks G3" to the `ingest` / `case create` / `tenant create` rows of the CLI table.
- **low** Phase 1.5 carries a relative commitment beside an absolute one (§ Phase 1.5 heading "committed: within 4 weeks of thesis validation" vs Release decision record "2026-11-30") — the two agree today only because the assumed dates are four weeks apart. If OQ1 moves the G1 date by SCP, the PRD does not say whether the L-gate date follows (relative) or holds (absolute). *Fix:* Drop the "within 4 weeks" clause or restate it as the rule for re-deriving the L-gate date when the G1 date changes.
