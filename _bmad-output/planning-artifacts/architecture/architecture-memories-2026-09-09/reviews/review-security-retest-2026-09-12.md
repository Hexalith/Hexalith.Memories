### Where the amendment is genuinely strong

Stated up front so the FAIL is not read as "nothing improved". AD-5's allowlist governance (`:92`) now **exceeds** what was asked: it splits principal identity (operator-owned, secret-scoped, versioned, no wildcards) from tenant scope (a tenant resource whose sole writer is AD-6's lifecycle workflow, revoked as an erasure completion condition). AD-7 (`:104`) states the case position once, unambiguously, and — critically — that declaration is what makes AD-12's handle rule and the new indistinguishable-error rule mutually consistent rather than merely coexisting (see Part B analysis under SEC-08). AD-16's property clause — "**every store that can hold tenant content or content-derived material**" — is the right shape for an erasure rule and survives the store-by-store walk below.

### SEC2-01 — critical — AD-5's revalidation clause has no lifecycle exemption, so tenant provisioning and tenant erasure cannot satisfy the rule that governs them

**Spine:** `:92` (three named obligations in one Rule: the three-check internal call requiring "that tenant being currently active and verified"; the background-work clause requiring revalidation "at each activity boundary" and failing closed "when the tenant is inactive, erased, or absent from the initiating principal's grant"; and the grant being "created only by verified provisioning and revoked as a completion condition of AD-16 erasure"), `:98` (AD-6 lifecycle workflows), `:158` (AD-16 erasure workflow), `:164` (AD-6-provisioned telemetry partition).

**What a compliant implementation permits.** There is no compliant implementation. The three clauses are mutually unsatisfiable at both ends of the tenant lifecycle, and the spine states no precedence between the background-work clause and the operator-principal clause that follows it four sentences later.

**Scenario, both ends.** *Provisioning:* AD-6's lifecycle workflow is work with no human caller. It must create the Redis ACL principal, the FalkorDB database, and — newly, per `:98` and `:164` — the tenant-isolated access-telemetry partition, which is a different service reached by an internal call. That internal call must satisfy three checks including "that tenant being currently active and verified", and must carry "an explicit grant for the requested tenant" — but `:92` says the grant "is created only by verified provisioning", which is the thing in progress. The provisioning workflow cannot authorize its own provisioning. *Erasure:* AD-16's workflow is likewise work with no human caller, carrying tenant authority, and it must revalidate "at each activity boundary" and fail closed "when the tenant is inactive **or erased**". The tenant becomes inactive at AD-6 deletion and erased partway through; `:92` additionally revokes the grant "as a completion condition" without fixing whether revocation precedes or follows the register write. On the safe reading, the erasure workflow halts before it can purge its remaining targets or write completion evidence, and FR39/NFR16 are unimplementable. On the live reading, implementers carve an unwritten exemption for "lifecycle work" — and AD-6's lifecycle scope includes **repair** (`:98`, `:92`: "Tenant creation, deletion, and lifecycle **repair**"), so the exemption lands squarely on the operation FR74 (`prd.md:1107`) requires to be tenant-bounded. An undocumented exemption to the only rule governing internal authorization is the single most valuable thing an adversary can find in this architecture, and the amendment created it.

**Closing text (AD-5 Rule, after the background-work sentence).** *"Tenant-lifecycle work is the one exception and is bounded here: a provisioning, deletion, erasure, or lifecycle-repair workflow authorized by an operator principal carries that operator authority for its whole duration, revalidates the operator principal rather than the tenant at each activity boundary, and is scoped at initiation to exactly one tenant identifier that it may not change on resume. It is exempt from the active-and-verified check and from grant containment only for the tenant it is scoped to, and only for the lifecycle operations enumerated in AD-6 and AD-16; every other internal call it makes satisfies all three checks. AD-16 erasure revokes the per-tenant grant only after the register carries completion evidence."*

### SEC2-02 — high — The erased-tenant register is now the single point of failure on every recovery path, with no store, no durability posture, no restore precedence, and no unavailability behaviour

**Spine:** `:158` (register created, "durable", "held outside any tenant-scoped keyspace", "sole authority for replay rejection, tenant-ID non-reuse, and restore admission", "written and owned by the erasure workflow"), `:292` (Operational Boundaries names it), `:344` (Deferred: the Dapr actor/workflow state store is the current Redis component, unqualified for durability and recovery), `:325` (ledger: the register is not maintained today).

**A compliant implementation permits this.** "Durable" and "retained indefinitely" are properties asserted of the record, not of a store. No AD assigns the register a home, and the predecessor's restore-precedence sentence was dropped in adoption.

**Scenario, four failure modes.** (a) *Silent regression:* the register is Dapr state on the Redis component `:344` calls unqualified. That component is restored from a pre-erasure snapshot after an incident. The register loses the erasure record, and the very restore that damaged it is then admitted by the check it just erased — the failure is self-concealing, because AD-16 made the register the *sole* authority, so no second signal contradicts it. (b) *Unavailability:* the register is unreachable during disaster recovery. AD-16 states no posture. Fail-closed blocks all recovery for all tenants on a single dependency; fail-open silently disables replay rejection, non-reuse, and restore admission at once. AD-5 says "fails closed" four times; AD-16 says it zero times. (c) *Bootstrap:* the register's only writer is the erasure workflow, so before the first erasure no register exists. "Any record whose tenant appears there" is trivially false against a missing store, and absence is indistinguishable from empty. (d) *Precedence:* AD-2 (`:74`) makes derived-store restore "an availability optimization"; nothing says the register outranks a restored store that disagrees with it.

**Closing text (AD-16 Rule, after "written and owned by the erasure workflow").** *"The register is held in a named store with a stated durability and recovery posture qualified independently of any tenant-scoped or projection store, is created at platform provisioning rather than at first erasure so that an empty register is distinguishable from a missing one, and is never restored from a snapshot older than the newest erasure it records. Replay, restore, and tenant creation fail closed when the register is unavailable, unreadable, or older than the data they would admit; that fail-closed state is an operator-visible condition with its own recovery procedure, not a silent pass."*

### SEC2-03 — high — AD-17's new authorization clause and AD-5's erased-tenant fail-closed rule make an erased tenant's retained telemetry permanently unpurgeable and unobservable

**Spine:** `:164` (AD-17: "Access-telemetry reads and writes derive tenant authority under AD-5 like any other surface"; Platform Operations owns "the configured TTL, observable purge progress"), `:92` (AD-5: background work "fails closed when the tenant is inactive, **erased**, or absent from the initiating principal's grant"; the per-tenant grant is "revoked as a completion condition of AD-16 erasure"), `:158` (AD-16 explicitly preserves retained telemetry: "those records follow AD-17's bounded TTL/purge contract"). **Requirement:** `prd.md:1202` (NFR34), `prd.md:546`.

**A compliant implementation permits this.** Each amendment is right alone. Together, AD-16 keeps records that AD-5 then forbids anyone from touching. Note this is the *inverse* of the risk the predecessor found: not content retained too long by accident, but content-free records retained **forever** because the mechanism that would remove them is now locked out.

**Scenario.** Tenant `claims` is erased. Its per-tenant grant is revoked (`:92`), the tenant is marked erased, and by AD-16's own design its access-telemetry records persist for the configured TTL. When the TTL expires, the purge job runs. That job is work with no human caller carrying tenant authority for tenant `claims`, so `:92` requires it to fail closed — the tenant is erased and no grant exists. The records never purge. Platform Operations cannot show "observable purge progress" for them for the same reason, and cannot exercise the erasure mapping, whose only purpose is to reach exactly these records. NFR34's TTL and purge-progress obligations become unexecutable for precisely the tenants that most need them, and `prd.md:546`'s "retained opaque access telemetry follows NFR34's TTL" becomes false.

**Closing text (AD-17 Rule).** *"Retention management of an erased tenant's records — TTL purge, purge-progress reporting, and use of the erasure mapping — is Platform Operations work authorized by an operator principal against the erased-tenant register, not by tenant authority, and is the one telemetry path exempt from AD-5's erased-tenant fail-closed rule. It may delete and count records and may never read their content-free fields into any product surface. Every other telemetry read and write follows AD-5."*

### SEC2-04 — high — AD-4 relocates tenant content to an "erasure-scoped store" that no decision names, provisions, authorizes, quotas, or registers

**Spine:** `:86` (AD-4: content "read from an **erasure-scoped store** at execution time and never persisted into workflow history or actor state" — the term appears once in the spine and is defined nowhere), `:110` (AD-8: provider work stays behind named adapters, portable coordination state uses Dapr, and only the finite Direct Redis Exception Registry may bypass Dapr state), `:98` (AD-6 owns tenant resource creation), `:158` (AD-16 purges "caches and derived artifacts"), `:202-210` (the Registry, whose sole row is the preflight reservation).

**A compliant implementation permits this.** The fix eliminated content from workflow history by moving it somewhere, and the somewhere has no owner. Two implementations comply: one re-derives extracted text from EventStore at each activity, the other writes it to an ad-hoc Redis blob keyed by workflow instance. Only the first has an isolation story.

**Scenario.** Tenant `claims` ingests a 40-page settlement PDF. The extract activity must hand its output to the embed activity without putting it in workflow history, so it writes the text to some store. That store is not EventStore (extracted text is derived, not domain truth, `:74`), not a product projection (it is intermediate), not Dapr coordination state (`:110` scopes that to coordination, and this is content), and not a Registry row. Consequently: AD-6 never provisions it, so it has no per-tenant principal and no tenant-scoped resource; AD-5 never authorizes reads of it; AD-18 never quotas it; AD-8's adapter boundary never contains it; AD-16 reaches it only if an implementer classifies it as a "cache or derived artifact". It is simultaneously the highest-value plaintext content store in the pipeline and the only content store the architecture does not name. It is also the natural place for the shared-credential exposure of ledger row `:324` to land.

**Closing text (AD-4 Rule, replacing "an erasure-scoped store").** *"…are read at execution time from a named intermediate content store that is a tenant resource under AD-6, reached only through an AD-8 named adapter, authorized under AD-5, bounded by a finite retention no longer than the ingestion workflow's own timeout, and enumerated as an AD-16 erasure target. Content-derived material may not be persisted anywhere the architecture has not named."* Add the store to AD-16's enumerated target list at `:158` and add a ledger row for its creation.

### SEC2-05 — high — Identifiers issued before AD-5's grammar existed are never validated, migrated, or ledgered

**Spine:** `:92` ("restrict tenant, case, and `MemoryUnitId` **issuance** to one documented bounded ASCII grammar"), `:189` (Conventions: values "are issued under AD-5's grammar"), `:314-336` (no ledger row for legacy identifiers), `:60` ("Where current code differs, the rule remains binding and the difference is implementation work" — with the ledger carrying the obligation, which here it does not). **Context:** `prd.md:896` records `TenantProvisioningWorkflow` as existing (Epic 0/5); `prd.md:997` records Epic 24 in progress.

**A compliant implementation permits this.** The rule binds issuance and nothing else. No validation-on-read, no re-issuance obligation, no quarantine of non-conforming identifiers, and — decisively — no ledger row, so AD-20 (`:182`) demands no owner and no evidence path.

**Scenario.** The platform has been provisioning tenants and cases since Epic 0. Every identifier issued before 2026-09-12 was accepted under "validated opaque token" with no character constraint. The grammar lands; new tenants are safe. A tenant provisioned earlier with an ID containing a Redis glob metacharacter keeps its ACL pattern, keeps its index names, and keeps the cross-tenant read described in the original SEC-01 — and the G2/NFR8 re-run that `prd.md:1141` schedules after Epic 24 will exercise *newly created* fixture tenants and pass. The isolation gate is evidenced on the population that was never at risk.

**Closing text (AD-5 Rule, after the grammar sentence).** *"The grammar is also a read-time invariant: any tenant, case, or MemoryUnitId that fails it is rejected wherever it is presented, and no derived key, ACL pattern, index name, or state key may be composed from a non-conforming identifier. Identifiers issued before this rule are enumerated, verified against the grammar, and either re-issued through AD-6's lifecycle or quarantined; that sweep is an active-foundation critical ledger row and its completion is part of G2 evidence."* Add the corresponding gap row.

### SEC2-06 — high — The grammar excludes characters but not case-folding collisions, and its system list omits three stores the spine itself mandates

**Spine:** `:92` (the exclusion list names "Redis ACL key patterns, Redis/RediSearch/FalkorDB key and index names, Dapr key and component names, URL path segments, or Kubernetes object names"), `:224` (PostgreSQL is the access-telemetry store), `:98` and `:164` (that store must have "a stated per-tenant principal or partition"), `:152` (per-tenant data-plane credentials as named secrets), `prd.md:1104` (FR71 export bundles).

**A compliant implementation permits this.** The rule is a *character* exclusion. Two of the four residual hazards are not about characters.

**Scenario, two variants.** (a) *Case folding.* Tenants `Acme` and `acme` contain only characters that are metacharacters nowhere, so both are legal under any bounded-ASCII grammar, and `:92`'s ordinal comparison makes them two distinct tenants. Kubernetes object names are DNS-1123 lowercase and unquoted PostgreSQL identifiers fold to lowercase — so if the AD-17 telemetry partition is a PostgreSQL schema or role named from the tenant ID, or any per-tenant Kubernetes object is, the two tenants collapse to one outside .NET. Tenant `Acme`'s access telemetry is written into tenant `acme`'s partition. No rule is broken; `:92` excluded characters, and case is not a character. (b) *Unlisted systems.* Three destinations the spine mandates are absent from the list: OpenBao/Dapr secret **path** segments (AD-15 requires per-tenant data-plane credentials as named secrets), PostgreSQL identifiers, and object-storage or filesystem path segments for FR71 export bundles. Each has its own reserved characters and normalization behaviour, and each carries an identifier the grammar was never checked against.

**Closing text (AD-5 Rule, extending the grammar sentence).** *"…or Kubernetes object names, OpenBao and Dapr secret path segments, SQL identifiers in any tenant-partitioned relational store, or object-storage and filesystem path segments. The grammar additionally admits a single case: identifiers are lowercase, so that ordinal distinctness in .NET is preserved by every downstream system that folds case. Two identifiers that differ only by case can never both be issued."*

### SEC2-07 — high — AD-5's pub/sub clause has a branch no subscriber can satisfy, splits the authorization decision across two writers, and omits the tenant-active check

**Spine:** `:92` (the pub/sub sentence, the allowlist's "exactly one writer, the operator, held in AD-15's operator secret scope", and the internal-call three checks including "that tenant being currently active and verified"), `:91` (*Prevents*: "two writers of one authorization decision"), `:190` (`/events/ingest`), `:198` (Environments: "Every environment uses the same Dapr component contracts").

**A compliant implementation permits this.** The clause offers two branches. Branch 1 — "the receiving component or topic is bound to one tenant" — requires per-tenant Dapr components or topics, which collides with `:98` (tenant resources are created by the lifecycle workflow, but Dapr components are deployment-time artifacts) and `:198` (identical component contracts across environments). So implementations take branch 2 — "the publishing app ID's grant must contain the envelope's tenant" — and branch 2 has no authenticated carrier.

**Scenario.** Phase 1.5 activates. A CloudEvent arrives at `/events/ingest`. The subscriber must determine the publishing app ID. Dapr pub/sub is decoupled: unlike service invocation, delivery carries no authenticated caller identity to the subscriber, only the envelope — whose `source` field is the very thing `:92` and AD-4 (`:86`) treat as *identity, never authority*. So a compliant implementer either reads `source` as authority (the original SEC-04 failure, re-entered through the fix) or relies on Dapr component `publishingScopes` — deployment-time component configuration that no AD names, editable by whoever edits component YAML, while the grant it is enforcing lives in the operator secret scope with a single named writer. That is two writers of one authorization decision, forbidden four lines earlier. Third gap: the pub/sub sentence requires channel binding or grant containment and **not** the "currently active and verified" check the sibling sentence requires, so a delivered event for an erased or deactivated tenant is admissible — re-ingesting content into a tenant AD-16 just erased.

**Closing text (AD-5 Rule, replacing the pub/sub sentence).** *"Dapr pub/sub ingress derives tenant authority from the delivery channel and never from the envelope. The channel is bound to a tenant set at deployment: the receiving component or topic is bound to one tenant, or the component's publishing scope restricts publishers to app IDs whose grants the operator artifact already contains, and that scope is generated from the allowlist artifact rather than maintained beside it — the allowlist remains the single writer. Envelope fields, including `source`, supply AD-4 identity only. An envelope whose tenant the channel does not authorize, or whose tenant is not currently active and verified, is rejected and not ingested."*

### SEC2-08 — medium — Export bundles are declared invalidated by erasure with no mechanism, and re-import under a different tenant ID matches no register entry

**Spine:** `:158` ("export bundles are erasure-scoped artifacts invalidated by their tenant's erasure"; the register check refuses "any record whose **tenant** appears there"), `:156` (*Binds*). **Requirement:** `prd.md:1159` (NFR16), `prd.md:1104` (FR71, shipped as Story 8.3).

**A compliant implementation permits this.** The predecessor proposed two mechanisms — registration of each bundle at creation, or wrapping bundles under the tenant key so shredding covers them — and the amendment adopted the conclusion without either. "Invalidated" is a state with no writer and no reader.

**Scenario.** A day-0 `memories export tenant --tenant claims` bundle is portable plaintext JSON sitting in object storage, a CI artifact, or a support ticket. Tenant `claims` is erased on day 1. The erasure workflow cannot invalidate the bundle because nothing records that it exists. On day 30 the bundle is re-imported. Under the same tenant ID the register rejects it. **Under a new tenant ID** — `claims-2`, a plausible and legitimate operator action after an erasure — every record presents tenant `claims-2`, which does not appear in the register, and the import proceeds. The content of the erased tenant is fully restored and searchable, and NFR16's "export restore … must never resurrect content from a tenant whose erasure completed" fails.

**Closing text (AD-16 Rule, replacing "export bundles are erasure-scoped artifacts invalidated by their tenant's erasure").** *"Export bundles are erasure-scoped artifacts: each is registered in the erased-tenant register's companion bundle index at creation with its tenant, issuance time, and content digest, and is either encrypted under the tenant key so shredding covers it or marked invalid by the erasure workflow. Import admits a bundle only when it is present in that index and not marked invalid, and re-keying a bundle to a different tenant identifier on import is forbidden; a bundle whose origin tenant appears in the register is refused whatever tenant identifier the import requests."*

### SEC2-09 — medium — "Verification" is procedural only, and the purge property is broader than the enumeration verification is bound to

**Spine:** `:158` (purge property: "**every store that can hold tenant content or content-derived material**", followed by an enumeration; then "Verification means a recorded, reproducible check **per enumerated target**"), `:326` (five direct-Redis coordination stores outside the adapter boundary). **Requirement:** `prd.md:1052` (FR39), `prd.md:1159` (NFR16: "Restore an erased-tenant fixture and prove zero content rehydrates"), `prd.md:546`.

**A compliant implementation permits this, two ways.** (a) The rule says what a check must **be** — recorded, reproducible, per target — and nothing about what it must **demonstrate**. Recording that each purge call returned success, per target, reproducibly, satisfies it exactly. The predecessor's substantive clause (content absent or unreadable per store; key material destroyed; a sampled ciphertext no longer decrypting) was dropped. (b) The purge obligation is property-scoped and the verification obligation is enumeration-scoped, so a store the property reaches and the enumeration omits — the failed-unit registry, the migration state, and the import leases of row `:326` are the live examples — is purged by obligation and verified by nobody. Completion evidence then lists a target set that is provably narrower than the purge set.

**Closing text (AD-16 Rule).** *"Verification means a recorded, reproducible check per target demonstrating a stated property: for each store, that no record keyed to or derived from the tenant remains readable; for the EventStore tenant key, that the key material is destroyed and a sampled ciphertext no longer decrypts; for the register, that the tombstone is present. The enumerated list is the minimum target set and the property clause governs its extension: adding a store that can hold tenant content or content-derived material adds an enumerated verification target in the same change, and completion evidence naming fewer targets than the property reaches is not completion."*

### SEC2-10 — medium — The indistinguishable-error convention collides with the mandatory actionable suggestion and with two versioned contract surfaces

**Spine:** `:192` (the same table row carries both "Errors carry stable code, human message, and **actionable suggestion**" and "Authorization and existence failures … return one indistinguishable response — same code, message, and timing class"; and "The error-code catalogue … is **append-only** under AD-12 … a code's meaning changes only in a versioned break"), `:193` (Active CLI output contract: "`CliExitCodes` and the JSON envelope are versioned contract surface under AD-12"; "Human, table, JSON, stderr, and exit-code forms preserve the same semantics"), `:134` (AD-12 additive evolution).

**A compliant implementation permits this.** The predecessor's closing sentence "the actionable suggestion is generic in that case" was dropped, leaving both obligations unconditional in one row, and the collapse itself was never reconciled with the row's own evolution rules.

**Scenario, three ways.** (a) An implementer honours the actionable-suggestion requirement literally and emits a suggestion specific enough to be useful — which is what distinguishes the two failures again, since the useful suggestion for not-authorized and for not-found differ. Nothing in the row resolves the precedence. (b) The error-code catalogue is a shipped `Contracts/V1` surface with, by construction, distinct not-authorized and not-found codes; making them indistinguishable is a *meaning* change to at least one existing code, which the same row permits "only in a versioned break", and AD-12's additive-evolution rule offers no path for a narrowing change. (c) `CliExitCodes` is versioned contract surface and `:193` requires identical semantics across every emitted form, so the exit codes must collapse too — a breaking change to a shipped map, with no versioned-break window budgeted, no name-register entry, and no ledger row. The security rule is right; the amendment landed it inside the one table row that forbids changing it.

**Closing text (Errors row, `:192`).** *"…and never name a scope, case, tenant, or resource the caller may not see; in that case the actionable suggestion is the generic recovery text for the collapsed code. Collapsing previously distinct authorization and existence codes into one indistinguishable code is a deliberate versioned break under AD-12, reserved in the name register and mirrored in `CliExitCodes` in the same change; until that break ships, surfaces emit the collapsed code and message with the pre-existing exit code reserved as an alias."*

### SEC2-11 — medium — Telemetry tenant isolation is a new obligation that accepts the weaker mechanism the ledger says cannot evidence NFR8, and carries no ledger row

**Spine:** `:98` (AD-6: Redis gets "a per-tenant ACL **principal**", FalkorDB "a separate tenant database/graph", and the access-telemetry store "a stated per-tenant principal **or partition**"), `:164` (AD-17 restates it), `:224` (PostgreSQL), `:324` (ledger, on exactly this reasoning: application-layer scoping means "G2/NFR8 cannot be evidenced at the boundary the requirement names"), `:314-336` (no row for the telemetry partition). **Requirement:** `prd.md:1141` (NFR8 is not scoped to product projections).

**A compliant implementation permits this.** "Or partition" admits one shared PostgreSQL credential with a tenant column — application-layer scoping under a different name. The spine argues against that mechanism for Redis in the row it classifies active-foundation critical, and permits it for the telemetry store in the same revision.

**Scenario.** The AccessTelemetry service holds every tenant's search and access history behind one database credential, tenant-scoped by a `WHERE tenant_id = @t` clause. AD-17's new authorization clause (`:164`) is honoured at the API boundary, so the scenario the predecessor named is closed; what remains is that a missing predicate, an admin query, a backup, or a read-replica exposes every tenant at once, with no second boundary underneath — the identical structure the ledger calls a Production blocker for Redis. Compounding it: provisioning and erasing the telemetry partition is now an AD-6 obligation with no gap row, so it has no owner, no evidence path, and — being unclassified — no AD-20 requirement to acquire one.

**Closing text.** In `:98`, replace "a stated per-tenant principal or partition" with *"a per-tenant principal, or a partition plus a stated dated expiry and Production-qualification block on the same footing as AD-15's Kubernetes-secret gap"*. Add a ledger row: *"The access-telemetry store has no per-tenant principal and no lifecycle-owned partition; provisioning and erasure of the telemetry partition are unimplemented. Violated rule: AD-6, AD-17. Active-foundation critical: Yes."*

### SEC2-12 — medium — Identifiers are content, and the two stores that retain them after erasure are the two AD-16 does not purge

**Spine:** `:164` (AD-17: records "carry tenant, case, principal, operation, outcome, and timing as **opaque or enumerated values**"), `:92` (the grammar constrains characters, not meaning — nothing requires an identifier to be non-descriptive), `:158` (AD-16 carves out access telemetry; its target list and *Binds* omit logs, traces, and metrics), `:197` (the amendment replaced "approved low-cardinality tenant representations" with "a documented, stable, **injective** mapping", dropping the cardinality constraint and thereby admitting the raw tenant ID as a label), `:291`.

**A compliant implementation permits this.** "Opaque" describes how a consumer must treat a value, not what the value contains. `henderson-v-acme-settlement` is an opaque string and also a fact about a legal matter.

**Scenario.** Tenant `claims` holds case `henderson-v-acme-settlement`. Access-telemetry records carry that case ID and the acting principal's identifier; they are content-free by AD-17's value-type test and are explicitly retained past erasure by AD-16 for the configured TTL, with the erasure mapping keeping them attributable to the erased tenant. Separately, `:197`'s amended rule permits the tenant ID itself as the metric and log label; the OpenTelemetry backend (`:230`) retains those series and log lines under its own retention policy, and logs, traces, and metrics appear in neither AD-16's target list nor its telemetry carve-out — they are simply outside the erasure contract. After a completed erasure the tenant's name, its case names, and its query volume over time remain queryable in the observability stack indefinitely.

**Closing text.** In AD-5 (`:92`), after the grammar sentence: *"Issued identifiers are non-descriptive: a tenant, case, or MemoryUnitId is a system-generated or operator-supplied token that carries no natural-language content, and the display name that carries meaning is tenant data subject to AD-16 erasure."* In AD-16 (`:158`), add to the target/carve-out treatment: *"Logs, traces, and metrics carry the AD-5 identifier or its injective label and no other tenant-derived value; after erasure they follow the same bounded retention and purge contract as access telemetry and are named in the completion evidence as a retained class."*

### SEC2-13 — medium — AD-4's content prohibition has no enforcement point and no ledger row, in a spine that elsewhere ledgers exactly this

**Spine:** `:86` (the prohibition), `:110` (AD-8 states its own boundary "is binding but currently unenforced and its convergence is ledgered" — the pattern this spine uses when a rule needs a guard), `:199` ("Architecture guards enforce dependency and literal ownership where they exist; the ledger carries the guards that do not"), `:314-336` (row `:325` covers *purging* workflow/actor state at erasure; no row covers *preventing* content from entering it), `:60`.

**A compliant implementation permits this.** The spine has an explicit convention for unenforced rules and did not apply it here. Content in workflow history is invisible at review time — it is a property of activity signatures and Durable Task serialization, not of a namespace — so without a guard the prohibition is discoverable only by inspecting the state store.

**Scenario.** Ingestion activities continue to pass extracted text and vectors as inputs and outputs. Nothing fails: no build guard, no architecture test, no ledger row, and no gate. The violation surfaces only when an erasure is audited, at which point the purge obligation of row `:325` catches it — but only for the tenant being erased, and only if the purge target list is applied to a store nobody expected to hold content.

**Closing text (AD-4 Rule, closing sentence).** *"This prohibition is enforced by an architecture test over activity and workflow input/output types and by an integration assertion that a completed ingestion workflow's history contains no content-derived field; until those exist the rule is binding but unenforced and its convergence is ledgered."* Add the corresponding ledger row at active-foundation critical.

### SEC2-15 — low — "Operator principal" is asserted throughout and derived nowhere

**Spine:** `:92` ("authorized by an operator principal, never by a tenant claim"; the allowlist "grants principal identity and never tenant scope"), `:152` ("operator scopes remain separate and least-privileged" — a *secret* scope), `:98`, `:158` (both depend on operator-authorized lifecycle work), `:288`.

**A compliant implementation permits this.** AD-5's four derivation paths yield a bearer principal with a tenant claim, a `system:*` principal with per-tenant grants, a channel-bound pub/sub tenant, and captured background authority. None is an operator principal, and the predecessor's two qualifying clauses — a derivation ("from the AD-5 allowlist or an equivalently derived operator authority") and a recording obligation ("the authorizing principal is recorded in the lifecycle evidence") — were both dropped in adoption. Every implementation invents an operator identity; nothing says what it is, where it comes from, or whether the erasure completion evidence names who ordered the erasure.

**Closing text (AD-5 Rule).** *"An operator principal is derived at the same authentication boundary as a user principal, is distinguished by an operator claim held in a separate issuer or role scope rather than by any tenant claim, is enumerated like the app-ID allowlist and changed by the same reviewed procedure, and is recorded by identity in the lifecycle and erasure evidence for every operation it authorizes."*

### SEC2-16 — low — Tenant-ID non-reuse is a new existence oracle the indistinguishable-error rule does not cover

**Spine:** `:158` (the register is "the sole authority for … tenant-ID non-reuse" and carries a tombstone with no TTL), `:192` (indistinguishability applies to "Authorization and existence failures on **scoped resources**" — a tenant creation is not a scoped-resource access).

**A compliant implementation permits this.** `memories tenant create --id acme` failing specifically on non-reuse discloses that a tenant `acme` once existed and was erased, indefinitely. Confined to operator principals, so the blast radius is small — but it is a disclosure the amendment introduced, and the indistinguishability rule was written before the register existed and does not reach it.

**Closing text (Errors row or AD-16).** *"Tenant-creation rejection for non-reuse returns the same code and message as any other unavailable-identifier rejection; only the erasure completion evidence, readable by an operator principal, distinguishes the two."*

### SEC2-17 — low — Three obligations were added with a stated bound and no owner

**Spine:** `:152` (AD-15: a rotated credential "is revoked at the provider **within a stated bound**" — the bound is stated nowhere, unlike AD-18's numeric budgets which `:170` routes to the PRD and validated configuration), `:92` (the allowlist is versioned and secret-scoped, so it is read through Dapr and cached — nothing bounds how long a removed app ID keeps working after the artifact changes; AD-15's rotation bound governs credentials, not authorization data), `:158` (register unavailability, per SEC2-02).

**Consequence.** Each is an obligation whose satisfaction is unfalsifiable, and none appears in the ledger, so AD-20 (`:182`) requires no owner and no evidence path for any of them. This is the class of item the amendment added most of.

**Closing text.** In AD-15 (`:152`), after "within a stated bound": *"that bound is a validated configuration value with a PRD-owned maximum, and the same bound governs propagation of a change to the AD-5 allowlist artifact: an app ID removed from the allowlist stops authorizing within it."* Add one ledger row covering the rotation bound, the allowlist propagation bound, and the register's durability and unavailability posture.

## Verified clean on re-test (checked adversarially, no finding)

- **AD-7 as the keystone (`:104`, `:134`, `:140`, `:192`).** Declaring case a non-authorization boundary is what makes the handle rule, the indistinguishable-error rule, AD-13's "authorize before explaining evidence", and AD-7's mandatory case attribution mutually consistent. Under the opposite choice all four would conflict. This is the strongest thing in the amendment.
- **Allowlist / grant split (`:92`).** Principal identity and tenant scope are separate artifacts with separate single writers, grants are created only by verified provisioning and revoked at erasure, wildcards require their own decision, and a miss fails closed with no default. It answers more than SEC-07 asked.
- **AD-16's purge property clause (`:158`).** "Every store that can hold tenant content or content-derived material" survives the store-by-store walk above. The defect is that verification is bound to the enumeration rather than the property (SEC2-09), not the property itself.
- **Restore admission decoupled from readability (`:158`).** "Regardless of whether the payload is readable" is the correct predicate and closes the projection-backup half of SEC-02 completely.
- **AD-15's rotation clause (`:152`).** Revocation, connection-lifetime bounding, and lifecycle verification that the prior credential no longer authenticates — all three variants of SEC-15 closed in one sentence.
- **AD-5's background-work clause (`:92`).** Revalidation at each activity boundary against active tenant state, with captured configuration explicitly denied authority, is a genuinely strong rule. Its only defect is over-reach into lifecycle work (SEC2-01).
- **AD-14 epoch ownership (`:146`)** and **injective tenant labels (`:197`)** — clean adoptions, no residue.
- **Direct Redis Exception Registry (`:202-210`)** — unchanged and still sound; the amendment added no rows and no bypasses.
